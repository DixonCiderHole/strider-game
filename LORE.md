# Strider: world and story bible

Reference for every datapad, radio log, line of dialogue, banter and quest.
Names marked *(proposed)* are placeholders until Matt signs them off.

## Tone

Sci-fi, futuristic and Star Wars-esque: a galactic war seen from the boots of
two stranded soldiers on a hostile frontier world. Gritty and grounded, with
small human moments.

## The war: two factions

**The Ascendancy** *(proposed)*: the "bad guys".
- An authoritarian galactic power ruled from the core, which believes order is earned by strength.
- Expanding its military and hyperspace network fast.
- Black and red armour, disciplined strike squads, heavy firepower.
- **The player character is an Ascendancy trooper** (confirmed).

**The Free Coalition** *(proposed)*: the "good guys".
- An alliance of independent worlds holding the line against the Ascendancy.
- Stretched thin, relies on recon, medics and resourcefulness.
- **The survivor (the woman in the game) is Coalition** (confirmed).

The two sides are fighting over who controls the galaxy. Neither came to this
planet for the planet itself.

## The planet: Kethra *(proposed name)*

- A primitive, forested frontier world. Until recently it was ignored: no
  advanced civilisation and no resources worth taking.
- **Why it matters now:** new hyperspace lanes (the **Meridian Run**
  *(proposed)*) drop out of hyperspace right over Kethra. Both militaries are
  expanding along them, so the planet has become a total **choke point and
  staging ground**. Whoever holds Kethra controls traffic through the sector.
- **The natives** (the **Vaari** *(proposed)*): **the main threat of the
  story.**
  - A primitive tribal people.
  - They don't discriminate between factions. To them every human is an
    invader, and they treat them that way.
  - **They are capturing humans and experimenting on them.** At first this is
    only hinted at, never shown outright. Hints to use:
    - bodies missing from crash sites and the trail
    - drag marks heading into the trees
    - empty restraints and cages
    - survey logs about crew who "walked into the forest"
    - creatures whose crystal growths look disturbingly deliberate
    - a Coalition dog tag found far from any wreck
  - *(open idea)* The spined creatures may be the Vaari's hunting beasts, or
    the products of their experiments.
- **Why ships keep falling: the crystal spires.**
  - The glowing crystal formations (the Crystal Spires landmark) form a
    planet-wide network.
  - The Vaari can "wake" the spires to send out a pulse that kills the drives
    of ships flying low over them.
  - It's primitive technology, if it's technology at all, and it's deadly.
  - This is why the player's ship was shot down, and the Coalition dropship
    too.
  - Survey teams found "something under the spires" (see datapad logs).
- **The creatures:** spined predators with crystalline growths along the
  spine. Their tissue reacts to light (they recoil from lamps and fire). They
  are linked to the spires, and to whatever the Vaari are doing to their
  captives.

## Why each side is here

**The player (Ascendancy):**
- Part of a strike squad sent after the **K-7 beacon** went silent: find out
  what happened to the first teams and get it running, so it guides the
  *Ascendancy* fleet in to **land and occupy Kethra**.
- Their ship was brought down by a spire pulse (the crash site). The squad
  survived the crash, then was **ambushed on the trail** in the clearing near
  the distress beacon. **He is the only survivor of his squad.**
- The player was pinned to a tree by a creature's spine.
- **Speciality: heavy weapons, mainly the flamethrower.** He **lost his
  heavy weapons in the crash** and starts with only a standard blaster.
  Getting his heavy gear back (salvaging or rebuilding a flamethrower) is a
  natural story and progression goal. The flamethrower also fits the world:
  the creatures recoil from fire and light.

**The survivor (Coalition):**
- A **field medic (corpsman)** with a Coalition pathfinder recon unit.
- Sent after the **K-7 beacon** went silent, to restore it first so it guides
  the *Coalition* fleet in to occupy the planet. Both sides were racing for
  the same beacon: it is the key to taking Kethra.
- Her dropship, the **HALCYON**, was downed by a spire pulse over the eastern
  ridge (this is the looping distress call on the camp radio).
- **Why you never see her ship:**
  - The HALCYON came down far away, beyond the **eastern ridge**: a two-day
    march through deep forest and Vaari territory.
  - Her unit salvaged what they could carry and set out on foot for K-7.
    Nothing worth going back for was left; the dropship burned out on impact.
  - When she says she "went back for supplies", she means her unit's packs at
    the ambush site, not her ship.
  - *(optional later twist)* If the player ever reaches the wreck, it has been
    stripped and dragged off by the Vaari: they take machines as well as
    people.
- Her unit survived the HALCYON crash and pushed towards K-7, but was
  **ambushed in the same area** as the player's squad. **She is the only
  survivor of her unit.**
- Both ambushes happened in the same stretch of forest, which suggests
  something there is hunting soldiers on purpose: the Vaari and their
  creatures. Bodies from both ambushes have gone missing (the experiments).
- She went back for medical supplies, found the player still breathing among
  the bodies on the trail, and dragged him to the cave.
- Name *(proposed)*: **Ilsa Renn**. Shown in game as SURVIVOR until she
  introduces herself.

**Survey Outpost K-7 and the beacon:**
- **The beacon was set up originally to signal the fleets.** Advance teams
  built the tower at K-7 so it would send a homing signal guiding a fleet
  safely down through the spire field.
- **Something went wrong.** The beacon went silent, and the initial teams
  were **never heard from again**, most likely taken by the Vaari for their
  experiments. The outpost was left half-abandoned. Its logs end mid-sentence,
  and the roster has crew with no "evacuated" mark.
- That silence is why both sides sent new squads (the player's strike squad
  and her recon unit): **find out what happened, get the beacon running, and
  guide their own fleet in to occupy Kethra.** Whoever controls the beacon
  controls the planet, so it is the central objective of the story.
- **Story twist:** when the two of them finally reach it, the beacon tower is
  **too destroyed to operate**. Nobody can guide a fleet in, and nobody is
  coming, for either side. They are **stranded together** on Kethra.
  This is a turning point for the relationship: the reason they were enemies
  (the race for the beacon) is gone, and only survival is left.
- Clues along the way can foreshadow the fate of the first teams: a K-7 log
  about "lights in the trees", personal effects left behind, and a final
  transmission cut off.

## Endgame: the Vaari facility

- **Near the end of the game** the player and the survivor find the Vaari's
  **main facility**. This is where they hold captured humans and experiment on
  them.
- The captives include the missing first teams from K-7 and bodies taken from
  both squads' ambush.
- **Why they're doing it is unknown for now.** Leave it a mystery, and decide
  the Vaari's goal later.
- Earlier hints (drag marks, empty cages, missing bodies, crystal growths on
  the creatures, cut-off logs) should all point towards this place.
- It should feel distinct from everything before it: alien, organic and
  crystal-lit, not the human sci-fi tech of the outposts and wrecks.

## The relationship arc

- **Early:** very uneasy. Enemies forced together to survive. Both lost their
  whole squads in the same ambush zone, and each half suspects the other's
  side had a hand in it.
  - She keeps her rifle close and the player's armour reminds her of what
    killed her friends.
  - The player doesn't understand why she saved them.
  - Short, guarded exchanges.
- **Middle:** grudging respect. They depend on each other in fights.
  - She patches them up.
  - They protect her with the heavy weapons.
  - Small personal details slip out.
- **Later:** trust. Real conversations about the war, their homeworlds, what
  each side told them about the other, what they'll do when rescue comes, and
  whose rescue it will be.

## Character notes

**The survivor (Coalition medic):**
- Practical and dry-humoured.
- Exhausted, and grieving her unit.
- Fiercely protective of the wounded, even an enemy.
- Hates waste.
- Talks to keep her hands steady.

**The player (Ascendancy heavy weapons):**
- Disciplined.
- Feels naked without his heavy weapons: a running thread for banter.
- Was taught that the Coalition are disorganised idealists.
- Has to square that with being kept alive by one.

**The Strider droid:**
- An escort unit.
- Loyal to whoever repairs it.
- Its black-box recorder may know more about the crash.

## Systems

**Banter / talk system** (built: `scripts/banter.gd`, `talk_point.gd`,
`dialogue_box.gd`):
- Look at a party member (Ilsa or the droid) and press E to talk.
- Each talk plays the next unlocked *topic* (a short conversation, heard once).
  A new topic comes up at most every 2.5 minutes; in between you get a
  one-line idle remark.
- Topics unlock by **trust stage**:
  - 0, wary: the start.
  - 1, grudging respect: quest 7+ or player level 6+.
  - 2, trust: after the beacon twist, flag `beacon_wrecked`.
  - Story scripts can force a stage with `GS.flags["trust"]`.
- A topic can also need a specific area, a story flag (`needs`), or the
  player to be hurt (`hurt`).
- **Not offered** while resting or sleeping, in cutscenes, in any open
  dialogue, with creatures within 30 m, or while `GS.flags["story_busy"]` is
  set (set this during story beats and pending story conversations).
- She's shown as SURVIVOR until her introduction topic; after that she's ILSA.
- Writing new banter: add entries to `TOPICS`/`IDLE` in `banter.gd`. Keep the
  early stages short and guarded, and let the later ones get personal.

**Quest starts:** future quests may begin by talking to someone or by
interacting with an object (datapad, terminal, beacon, body and so on), not
only automatically.
